Pharmaceutical Organic Chemistry II syllabus (BP301T), unit-wise

  1. Unit 1 · Benzene and its derivatives

    Analytical, synthetic and other evidences for the structure of benzene; orbital picture; resonance; aromatic character and Hückel's rule. Reactions of benzene: nitration, sulphonation, halogenation, Friedel–Crafts alkylation (limitations) and acylation. Effect of substituents on reactivity and orientation in electrophilic substitution. Structure and uses of DDT, saccharin, BHC and chloramine.

  2. Unit 2 · Phenols, aromatic amines and aromatic acids

    Phenols: acidity, effect of substituents on acidity, qualitative tests; structure and uses of phenol, cresols, resorcinol, naphthols. Aromatic amines: basicity, effect of substituents on basicity, synthetic uses of aryl diazonium salts. Aromatic acids: acidity, effect of substituents, important reactions of benzoic acid.

  3. Unit 3 · Fats and oils

    Fatty acids and their reactions; hydrolysis, hydrogenation, saponification, rancidity of oils, drying oils. Analytical constants: acid value, saponification value, ester value, iodine value, acetyl value, Reichert–Meissl (RM) value: significance and principle of determination.

  4. Unit 4 · Polynuclear hydrocarbons

    Synthesis and reactions; structure and medicinal uses of naphthalene, phenanthrene, anthracene, diphenylmethane, triphenylmethane and their derivatives.

  5. Unit 5 · Cycloalkanes

    Stabilities: Baeyer's strain theory and its limitations, Coulson and Moffitt's modification, Sachse–Mohr theory (strainless rings). Reactions of cyclopropane and cyclobutane only.

Pharmaceutical Organic Chemistry II MCQ with answers

Q1. The resonance energy of benzene obtained from heats of hydrogenation is about:

  1. 36 kcal/mol
  2. 28.6 kcal/mol
  3. 49.8 kcal/mol
  4. 85.8 kcal/mol
Show answer

Answer: (a) 36 kcal/mol. Expected 85.8 (3 x 28.6) minus observed 49.8 = about 36 kcal/mol.

Q2. Which of the following is NOT aromatic?

  1. Tropylium cation
  2. Cyclooctatetraene
  3. Cyclopentadienyl anion
  4. Pyridine
Show answer

Answer: (b) Cyclooctatetraene. Cyclooctatetraene has 8 pi electrons and is tub-shaped (non-planar), so it is non-aromatic.

Q3. Which is the strongest acid?

  1. Phenol
  2. p-Cresol
  3. m-Nitrophenol
  4. p-Nitrophenol
Show answer

Answer: (d) p-Nitrophenol. -NO2 at para stabilises phenoxide by resonance and induction; at meta only by induction.

Q4. Phenol on treatment with CHCl3 and aqueous NaOH followed by acidification gives:

  1. Salicylaldehyde
  2. Salicylic acid
  3. Benzaldehyde
  4. Phenyl chloroformate
Show answer

Answer: (a) Salicylaldehyde. Reimer-Tiemann reaction; the electrophile is dichlorocarbene.

Q5. Reichert-Meissl value is defined for how much fat?

  1. 1 g
  2. 10 g
  3. 5 g
  4. 100 g
Show answer

Answer: (c) 5 g. RM value: ml of 0.1 N alkali for volatile water-soluble acids from 5 g fat.

Q6. Iodine value of a drying oil is:

  1. Below 100
  2. 100-130
  3. Exactly zero
  4. Above 130
Show answer

Answer: (d) Above 130. Drying oils such as linseed oil are highly unsaturated, IV above 130.

Q7. Haworth synthesis of naphthalene starts with benzene and:

  1. Phthalic anhydride
  2. Succinic anhydride
  3. Maleic anhydride
  4. Acetic anhydride
Show answer

Answer: (b) Succinic anhydride. Benzene + succinic anhydride (AlCl3) gives beta-benzoylpropionic acid, the first intermediate.

Q8. Sulphonation of naphthalene at about 160 C mainly gives:

  1. Naphthalene-1-sulphonic acid
  2. Naphthalene-1,5-disulphonic acid
  3. Naphthalene-2-sulphonic acid
  4. Phthalic acid
Show answer

Answer: (c) Naphthalene-2-sulphonic acid. High temperature favours the thermodynamically more stable beta (2-) isomer.

Q9. According to Baeyer, angle strain (deviation) in cyclobutane is:

  1. 24 deg 44'
  2. 9 deg 44'
  3. 0 deg 44'
  4. -5 deg 16'
Show answer

Answer: (b) 9 deg 44'. d = 1/2 (109 deg 28' - 90 deg) = 9 deg 44'.

Q10. Baeyer's strain theory predicted the most stable ring to be:

  1. Cyclopropane
  2. Cyclobutane
  3. Cyclopentane
  4. Cyclohexane
Show answer

Answer: (c) Cyclopentane. Cyclopentane has the smallest deviation (0 deg 44') for a planar ring.

Sample model answers

How a good exam answer for Pharmaceutical Organic Chemistry II looks (sample answers; the full set opens in the daily practice).

[2 marks] State Huckel's rule.

Huckel's rule: a monocyclic, planar compound in which every ring atom has a p orbital (complete conjugation) is aromatic if it contains (4n+2) pi electrons, where n = 0, 1, 2, 3... (2, 6, 10, 14 pi electrons). Example: benzene has 6 pi electrons (n = 1).

[2 marks] Why is sulphonation of benzene reversible? Give one application of this.

In sulphonation the energy barriers for forward and backward reactions are nearly equal, so an equilibrium exists. When benzenesulphonic acid is heated with dilute H2SO4 or superheated steam, the SO3H group is lost and benzene is regenerated (desulphonation). Application: SO3H is used as a temporary blocking group to direct another group to a desired position, then removed.

[10 marks] Why is benzene aromatic? Explain Huckel's rule with examples of aromatic, antiaromatic and non-aromatic compounds.

Aromaticity is the unusual stability of certain cyclic, planar, conjugated molecules containing (4n+2) delocalised pi electrons. Benzene (C6H6) is the parent aromatic compound.

  • Why benzene is aromatic (1) - structure: all six carbons are sp2 hybridised; the ring is a flat regular hexagon with 120 degree angles.
  • Why benzene is aromatic (2) - complete conjugation: each carbon has an unhybridised p orbital; the six p orbitals overlap continuously round the ring forming pi clouds above and below the plane.
  • Why benzene is aromatic (3) - electron count: benzene has 6 pi electrons, which fits 4n+2 with n = 1.
  • Aromatic characters of benzene: high resonance energy (about 36 kcal/mol from heats of hydrogenation, 49.8 observed vs 85.8 expected), equal C-C bond lengths of 1.39 A, preference for electrophilic substitution over addition, resistance to oxidation, and a ring current giving downfield NMR signals (about 7.3 ppm).
  • Huckel's rule: a monocyclic, planar, fully conjugated compound is aromatic if it has (4n+2) pi electrons, n = 0, 1, 2, 3 (i.e. 2, 6, 10, 14...).
  • Aromatic examples: benzene (6), naphthalene (10), anthracene and phenanthrene (14), cyclopropenyl cation (2), cyclopentadienyl anion (6), tropylium (cycloheptatrienyl) cation (6), and heterocycles such as pyridine, pyrrole, furan and thiophene (6).
  • Antiaromatic compounds: cyclic, planar, conjugated with 4n pi electrons; they are less stable than open-chain analogues. Example: cyclobutadiene (4 pi electrons), cyclopentadienyl cation (4).
  • Non-aromatic compounds: fail any condition, e.g. cyclooctatetraene (8 pi electrons) is tub-shaped and non-planar so it behaves like an ordinary polyene; cyclohexene and cyclopentadiene lack complete conjugation.

Diagram: Draw benzene as a hexagon with a circle inside; beside it draw the p orbital picture (six vertical p orbitals on a flat hexagon merging into a pi cloud above and below). Also draw cyclopentadienyl anion, tropylium cation, cyclobutadiene (square) and tub-shaped cyclooctatetraene with their pi electron counts.

Conclusion: Benzene is aromatic because it is cyclic, planar, fully conjugated and has 6 pi electrons obeying Huckel's (4n+2) rule, which gives it large resonance stabilisation.
