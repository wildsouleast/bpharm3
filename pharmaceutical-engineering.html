Pharmaceutical Engineering syllabus (BP304T), unit-wise

  1. Unit 1 · Flow of fluids, size reduction and size separation

    Manometers, Reynolds number, Bernoulli's theorem, energy losses; orifice meter, venturimeter, pitot tube, rotameter. Size reduction: laws, factors; hammer mill, ball mill, fluid energy mill, edge runner and end runner mill. Size separation: official grades of powders, standard sieves; sieve shaker, cyclone separator, air separator, bag filter, elutriation tank.

  2. Unit 2 · Heat transfer, evaporation and distillation

    Mechanisms of heat transfer, Fourier's law, conduction, convection, radiation, heat exchangers and interchangers. Evaporation: factors; steam jacketed kettle, horizontal tube, climbing film, forced circulation and multiple effect evaporators (economy). Distillation: simple, flash, fractional, reduced pressure, steam and molecular distillation.

  3. Unit 3 · Drying and mixing

    Drying: mechanism, equilibrium moisture content, rate of drying curve; tray, drum, spray, fluidized bed, vacuum and freeze dryers. Mixing: factors, solid vs liquid mixing, mechanisms; double cone, twin shell and ribbon blenders, sigma blade and planetary mixers, propellers, turbines, paddles, Silverson emulsifier.

  4. Unit 4 · Filtration and centrifugation

    Filtration: theories, factors, filter aids and media; plate and frame filter, filter leaf, rotary drum filter, meta filter, cartridge filter, membrane filter, Seitz filter. Centrifugation: principle and applications; perforated and non-perforated basket, semi-continuous and super centrifuge.

  5. Unit 5 · Materials of plant construction, corrosion and material handling

    Factors in selecting materials for pharmaceutical plants; theories and types of corrosion and their prevention; ferrous and non-ferrous metals, inorganic and organic non-metals; basics of material handling systems.

Pharmaceutical Engineering MCQ with answers

Q1. Reynolds number is the ratio of:

  1. Inertial forces to viscous forces
  2. Viscous forces to gravity forces
  3. Pressure forces to inertial forces
  4. Surface tension to viscous forces
Show answer

Answer: (a) Inertial forces to viscous forces. Re = Duρ/μ = inertial force / viscous force; it is dimensionless.

Q2. Bernoulli's theorem is based on the law of conservation of:

  1. Mass
  2. Energy
  3. Momentum
  4. Charge
Show answer

Answer: (b) Energy. Total energy (pressure + kinetic + potential) of an ideal fluid in steady flow remains constant.

Q3. Fourier's law is the basic law of heat transfer by:

  1. Conduction
  2. Convection
  3. Radiation
  4. Condensation
Show answer

Answer: (a) Conduction. dQ/dθ = -kA dt/dx describes conduction through a solid.

Q4. According to the Stefan-Boltzmann law, energy radiated by a black body is proportional to:

  1. T
  2. T⁴
  3. T²
  4. T³
Show answer

Answer: (b) T⁴. E = σT⁴, where T is absolute temperature.

Q5. During the constant rate period of drying, the surface of the solid is:

  1. Completely dry
  2. Covered with a crust
  3. At the dry-bulb air temperature
  4. Saturated with water
Show answer

Answer: (d) Saturated with water. Unbound water keeps the surface saturated, so evaporation is like from a free water surface.

Q6. Critical moisture content is the moisture content at which:

  1. The constant rate period ends and falling rate begins
  2. Drying begins
  3. Equilibrium is reached
  4. The solid starts to melt
Show answer

Answer: (a) The constant rate period ends and falling rate begins. After CMC the surface is no longer fully wet and the rate falls.

Q7. The equation that relates filtration rate to the porosity (ε) and specific surface of the cake is:

  1. Poiseuille's equation
  2. Darcy's equation
  3. Kozeny-Carman equation
  4. Stokes' equation
Show answer

Answer: (c) Kozeny-Carman equation. Kozeny-Carman: rate ∝ ε³ / [(1-ε)² S²], showing the strong effect of porosity and particle size.

Q8. According to Poiseuille's equation, flow rate through a capillary is proportional to:

  1. r
  2. r²
  3. r³
  4. r⁴
Show answer

Answer: (d) r⁴. dV/dt = πr⁴ΔP / 8ηL; doubling pore radius increases flow 16 times.

Q9. Corrosion resistance of stainless steel is mainly due to:

  1. Carbon content
  2. A zinc coating
  3. High nickel only
  4. A passive chromium oxide film
Show answer

Answer: (d) A passive chromium oxide film. Chromium (at least about 11-12%) forms a thin, self-healing passive oxide layer.

Q10. SS 316 differs from SS 304 mainly by the presence of:

  1. Molybdenum
  2. Copper
  3. Lead
  4. Tin
Show answer

Answer: (a) Molybdenum. About 2-3% molybdenum improves resistance to chlorides and pitting, so SS 316 is preferred for product-contact parts.

Sample model answers

How a good exam answer for Pharmaceutical Engineering looks (sample answers; the full set opens in the daily practice).

[2 marks] What is the significance of the Reynolds number?

Reynolds number (Re = Duρ/μ) is the dimensionless ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces. Its significance is that it predicts the type of flow: Re below 2000 = laminar (streamline) flow, 2000-4000 = transition, above 4000 = turbulent flow. It is used in design of pipes, pumps and mixers and to calculate friction losses.

[2 marks] Define sieve number.

Sieve number is the number of meshes (openings) present in a length of 2.54 cm (one inch) in each transverse direction parallel to the wires of the sieve. A higher sieve number means smaller openings, i.e. a finer sieve (e.g. sieve no. 120 is finer than sieve no. 10).

[10 marks] Explain the principle, construction, working, uses, merits and demerits of hammer mill and ball mill with neat diagrams.

Hammer mill and ball mill are commonly used size reduction equipment. The hammer mill works by impact, while the ball mill works by impact and attrition.

  • HAMMER MILL - Principle: impact. Material is hit by rapidly rotating hammers and shattered against the casing.
  • Construction: a steel casing containing a central shaft (rotor) on which four or more swinging hammers are attached. A screen (sieve) of desired size is fitted at the bottom of the casing. Hammers may be blunt (impact, for brittle material) or knife-edged (cutting, for fibrous material).
  • Working: the rotor rotates at high speed (about 2500-10000 rpm). Material fed from the top hopper is struck by the hammers and thrown against the casing. It stays in the mill until it is small enough to pass through the screen. Product size depends on rotor speed, screen size and feed rate.
  • Uses: grinding dried granules, crude drugs, chemicals, wet masses (for granulation) and fibrous material (with knife edge).
  • Merits: rapid, compact, easy to clean, continuous operation, product size controlled by changing the screen, versatile.
  • Demerits: heat generated may damage thermolabile drugs, screen may clog with sticky material, wear of hammers and screens causes contamination, not for very hard abrasive materials.
  • BALL MILL - Principle: impact and attrition. At proper speed the balls are lifted and fall (cascade) on the material (impact), and rub against each other (attrition).
  • Construction: a hollow cylinder (steel, porcelain or stoneware) rotating on its horizontal axis, about 30-50% filled with balls of steel, porcelain or flint. Material fills the voids between the balls.
  • Working: at low speed the balls only roll (attrition, little grinding); at too high speed (critical speed) the balls centrifuge with the wall and no grinding occurs. At optimum speed (one-half to three-fourth of critical speed, Nc = 0.5/√(R - r) rps) the balls cascade and give maximum size reduction.
  • Uses: fine grinding of dyes, pigments, chemicals and crude drugs; wet grinding of suspensions; milling of toxic or sterile materials in a closed cylinder.
  • Merits: produces very fine powder, wet and dry grinding both possible, closed system (no dust, sterile and toxic material), low installation cost, continuous or batch.
  • Demerits: slow process, very noisy, wear of balls and cylinder can contaminate product, not suitable for soft, sticky or fibrous materials.

Diagram: Hammer mill: a casing with a central rotor carrying swinging hammers, feed hopper at top, screen at bottom and product outlet below. Ball mill: horizontal cylinder partly filled with balls, showing three positions of balls - sliding at low speed, cascading at optimum speed, centrifuging at critical speed.

Conclusion: Hammer mill suits rapid grinding of brittle and fibrous drugs, while ball mill suits fine grinding in a closed system; both are standard pharmaceutical mills.
