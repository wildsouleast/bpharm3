Pharmaceutical Microbiology syllabus (BP303T), unit-wise

  1. Unit 1 · Introduction, bacteria, culture and microscopy

    History, branches and scope of microbiology; prokaryotes vs eukaryotes; ultrastructure and morphological classification of bacteria; nutritional needs, culture media and growth conditions; growth curve; isolation and preservation of pure cultures; cultivation of anaerobes; total and viable counts. Phase contrast, dark field and electron microscopy.

  2. Unit 2 · Staining, IMViC and sterilization

    Simple, Gram's and acid-fast staining; IMViC tests. Physical, chemical and mechanical sterilization: principle, procedure, merits, demerits, applications. Evaluating sterilization efficiency, large-scale sterilization equipment, sterility indicators.

  3. Unit 3 · Fungi, viruses, disinfectants and sterility testing

    Morphology, classification, reproduction/replication and cultivation of fungi and viruses. Classification and mode of action of disinfectants; factors influencing disinfection; evaluation of antiseptics (bactericidal and bacteriostatic). Sterility testing of products as per IP, BP and USP.

  4. Unit 4 · Aseptic area and microbiological assay

    Design of an aseptic area, laminar air flow, sources of contamination and their prevention, clean area classification. Principles and methods of microbiological assay; standardization of antibiotics, vitamins and amino acids; assessment of a new antibiotic.

  5. Unit 5 · Spoilage, preservation and cell culture

    Types of spoilage, factors affecting microbial spoilage, sources and types of contaminants, assessment of spoilage. Preservation with antimicrobial agents and evaluation of microbial stability. Animal cell culture: general procedure, primary, established and transformed cell lines, applications in industry and research.

Pharmaceutical Microbiology MCQ with answers

Q1. Ribosomes found in the cytoplasm of bacteria are of which type?

  1. 70S
  2. 60S
  3. 90S
  4. 80S
Show answer

Answer: (a) 70S. Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes (50S + 30S); eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S.

Q2. Teichoic acid is a characteristic component of the cell wall of:

  1. Fungi
  2. Gram-positive bacteria
  3. Mycoplasma
  4. Gram-negative bacteria
Show answer

Answer: (b) Gram-positive bacteria. Teichoic and lipoteichoic acids are found in the thick peptidoglycan wall of Gram-positive bacteria.

Q3. In Gram staining, the step that differentiates Gram-positive from Gram-negative bacteria is:

  1. Safranin counterstain
  2. Decolourisation with alcohol/acetone
  3. Crystal violet
  4. Gram's iodine
Show answer

Answer: (b) Decolourisation with alcohol/acetone. Decolouriser removes the crystal violet-iodine complex only from Gram-negative cells.

Q4. The IMViC pattern of Escherichia coli is:

  1. --++
  2. -+-+
  3. ++--
  4. +-+-
Show answer

Answer: (c) ++--. E. coli is indole +, methyl red +, Voges-Proskauer -, citrate -.

Q5. The cell wall of fungi is mainly composed of:

  1. Teichoic acid
  2. Cellulose only
  3. Peptidoglycan
  4. Chitin and glucans
Show answer

Answer: (d) Chitin and glucans. Fungal walls contain chitin and glucans; peptidoglycan is bacterial.

Q6. Candida albicans is best described as a:

  1. Yeast-like fungus forming pseudohyphae
  2. Dimorphic mould
  3. True mould
  4. Actinomycete
Show answer

Answer: (a) Yeast-like fungus forming pseudohyphae. Candida reproduces by budding and forms pseudohyphae, so it is called yeast-like.

Q7. A HEPA filter removes at least what percentage of 0.3 µm particles?

  1. 99.97%
  2. 100%
  3. 95%
  4. 99%
Show answer

Answer: (a) 99.97%. HEPA filters are rated at 99.97% efficiency for 0.3 µm particles.

Q8. Grade A clean area corresponds approximately to:

  1. ISO 5
  2. ISO 7
  3. ISO 9
  4. ISO 8
Show answer

Answer: (a) ISO 5. Grade A (and Grade B at rest) correspond to ISO 5, the old Class 100.

Q9. Benzoic acid is an effective preservative only at acidic pH because:

  1. It degrades in alkaline pH
  2. It reacts with water
  3. Only its unionised form is antimicrobial
  4. It precipitates at neutral pH
Show answer

Answer: (c) Only its unionised form is antimicrobial. Weak acid preservatives act in the unionised form, which predominates below their pKa (about 4.2).

Q10. Parabens lose preservative activity in the presence of:

  1. Sucrose at low levels
  2. Sodium chloride
  3. Citric acid
  4. Polysorbate 80
Show answer

Answer: (d) Polysorbate 80. Parabens bind to non-ionic surfactant micelles, lowering the free concentration.

Sample model answers

How a good exam answer for Pharmaceutical Microbiology looks (sample answers; the full set opens in the daily practice).

[2 marks] Define microbiology. Write its branches and scope in pharmacy.

Microbiology is the study of microorganisms (bacteria, fungi, viruses, protozoa, algae) that are too small to be seen with the naked eye.

Branches: bacteriology, virology, mycology, protozoology, immunology; applied branches like medical, industrial, food, environmental and pharmaceutical microbiology.

Scope in pharmacy: production of antibiotics, vaccines, enzymes and vitamins by fermentation; sterilization and sterility testing of products; microbiological assay of antibiotics and vitamins; control of contamination and spoilage; preservative evaluation; biotechnology and cell culture.

[2 marks] Write a short note on the history of microbiology (contributions of Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur and Koch).

Antonie van Leeuwenhoek (17th century) made simple microscopes and first described microorganisms, calling them 'animalcules'; he is called the father of microbiology/bacteriology.

Louis Pasteur disproved spontaneous generation using swan-neck flasks, introduced pasteurization, studied fermentation and developed vaccines (anthrax, rabies).

Robert Koch proved the germ theory of disease, gave Koch's postulates, isolated anthrax, tubercle and cholera bacilli and introduced solid media.

Joseph Lister introduced antiseptic surgery using carbolic acid (phenol).

[10 marks] Describe the ultrastructure of a bacterial cell with a neat labelled diagram.

A bacterial cell is a prokaryotic cell of about 1-10 µm. Its structures are grouped as external (capsule, flagella, pili), cell envelope (cell wall, cytoplasmic membrane) and internal (cytoplasm, ribosomes, nucleoid, inclusions, spores).

  • Capsule/slime layer: polysaccharide or polypeptide layer outside the wall; protects from phagocytosis and drying; a virulence factor (e.g. Pneumococcus).
  • Flagella: long thin protein (flagellin) appendages for motility; arrangements monotrichous, lophotrichous, amphitrichous, peritrichous.
  • Pili/fimbriae: short hair-like protein structures for attachment; sex pili transfer DNA in conjugation.
  • Cell wall: rigid peptidoglycan layer giving shape and protection from osmotic lysis; thick with teichoic acid in Gram-positive; thin with outer LPS membrane in Gram-negative.
  • Cytoplasmic membrane: phospholipid bilayer with proteins; selective permeability, transport, respiration enzymes (bacteria lack mitochondria).
  • Mesosomes: infoldings of the membrane, thought to help in respiration and cell division.
  • Cytoplasm: aqueous gel containing enzymes, nutrients and 70S ribosomes (50S + 30S) for protein synthesis.
  • Nucleoid: single circular double-stranded DNA without nuclear membrane; plasmids are small extra-chromosomal DNA carrying genes like drug resistance.
  • Inclusion bodies: storage granules such as volutin (metachromatic granules), glycogen, poly-beta-hydroxybutyrate.
  • Endospores: highly resistant dormant structures formed by Bacillus and Clostridium; resist heat and disinfectants, hence important in sterilization.

Diagram: Draw an oval/rod cell. Label from outside in: capsule, cell wall, cytoplasmic membrane, mesosome (infolding), cytoplasm with ribosomes (dots), nucleoid (coiled DNA), plasmid (small ring), inclusion granules, flagellum (long whip at one end), pili (short hairs on surface).

Conclusion: Knowing bacterial structure explains Gram staining, antibiotic targets (cell wall, ribosomes) and resistance of spores to sterilization.
